Q2 release: 75,472 including ~8,000 impacted by the May reduction, "the majority of whom will no
longer be reflected in our headcount by the end of Q3", minus underlying net attrition. The book
priced the 68k leg at 0.675, which assumed most of those people were still counted.

The four OVV legs were all No-side and all won as counterfactuals. `core/counterfactual.py
ledger --rows`: +10.15, +2.25, +1.49, +1.17 = **+$15.06 forgone** at $5 flat. These are 4 legs
of one correlated event, so the realizable figure is lower. Under the $10 first-contact family
cap, the two best legs (68k and 70k) would have made about +$12.40.

What went right: the arithmetic took the issuer's forward statement literally, and the issuer
delivered what it said. This is the "issuer-guided count" shape: a company states a reduction in
a count it will itself publish, and the market has not priced the statement.

What the veto did: the fact-finality gate correctly says the Sep 30 count was not published at
record time. The gate is there because "benchmark-derived" edges > 0.10 lose. This family is
n=1 event, so it does not overturn the gate. Logged as the first data point for an
issuer-guided-count carve-out, pre-registered in the playbook (below).
